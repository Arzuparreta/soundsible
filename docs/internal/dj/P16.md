# P16. Per-deck continuity and connection recovery

Physical acceptance (2026-10-30): the user confirms that the changes
work as expected in their real listening.

Original implementation: PR #408. Physical acceptance confirmed by the user
(2026-10-30). The November 7 follow-up described below requires new
acceptance on the iPhone.

## Evidence

Trip of 2026-10-30: 1,190 iPhone events, without sequence gaps,
playback approximately between 15:27 and 15:46 Europe/Madrid.
Only the diagnosis is kept here; the private databases stay outside
the repository.

- "Ambitions" → "The Parting", 15:31: loss of approximately one second
  of progress during speed changes; after removing the outgoing track,
  a 163 ms step back when returning to 1×.
- "The Parting" → "Old Heart Falls", 15:35: 44 ms step back and clock
  stopped during part of the speed return. Both incoming tracks had

- P15 detects both failures in the return, but during the mix it only
  watched individual steps back >50 ms and four-second stalls.
  A clock that keeps advancing irregularly escaped that supervision.
- The user also hears an incoming track that is absent until the outgoing one ends.
  The recorded counters advance: without measuring PCM and effective gain
  that silence cannot be located or attributed with certainty.
- The engine was not restarted. The PWA identification responded correctly
  at 15:27:08; there is no evidence of the failure before the authenticated startup.
  Startup without identification was left waiting for a manual retry and the
  socket abandoned its reconnection after five attempts.

## Responsibilities

`audio/mediaClock.ts` measures renderer continuity: it integrates the requested
speed and compares progress with monotonic time. It detects an accumulated step back
>50 ms; a deficit >250 ms must repeat in two consecutive complete windows,
of at least one second each, to confirm `clock_starved`. A first
deficient window only establishes suspicion. A healthy window clears it: an
initial offset that later stops growing is not persistent starvation. The
margin allows one complete update quantum of the element. Pauses,
seeks, lack of data and supervision gaps >2 s also reset the suspicion.
It does not measure or recalibrate musical confidence. Mixer, speed return and
transport supervision share this observer.

`audio/signal.ts` observes PCM RMS in windows of 256 samples, per channel
(the highest RMS is kept so that antiphase does not look like silence). `graph.ts`
owns one meter before effects/leveling and another after the per-deck
fade. It returns unknown if the API does not exist or the context is not
rendering. Zero means observed digital silence, not a demonstrated failure
of the file; a positive signal does not certify the car speakers either.
No samples, audio, URLs or titles are stored in the trace.

`mixer.ts` keeps the ownership of each transition and its fallbacks:

- Curves, dominance and removal use the same Web Audio clock when there is a
  graph. The file progress still supervises continuity and phase, but
  a speed correction does not move the removal earlier or later relative to the
  fade. Without a graph, gains and dominance share the file progress.
- It only opens the fade with recent progress, data and, when it can be measured,
  PCM signal from the incoming track. It also requires signal after the fader before
  transferring dominance. A missing measurement API keeps the
  clock and availability path; it is never interpreted as silence.
- If B loses the signal before acquiring dominance, it restores A, cancels its
  effects and keeps B prepared for the ordinary handoff at the end of A.
  A failed mix therefore does not remove a valid song from the route.
- If B already owns the program, A covers the interruption while it is still
  available. The fade and its effects stop running toward silence; when
  B delivers signal again, a simple fade of up to four
  seconds is opened with a new origin. Dominance does not go back and its
  callback is not repeated. The natural end of A keeps the existing handoff protocol
  and the ordinary recovery of B.
- A clock failure while varying speed returns the deck to 1× and disables
  speed control for that audio runtime. The following mixes
  run `structural_fade` at 1×, leaving evidence of the degradation. A
  new audio session can try again. The shared analysis
  and the technique chosen by the server for other devices are not modified.
  If the failure already occurs at 1×, it protects that mix without concluding that speed
  control is incompatible and without disabling it for the following ones.
- Phase corrections do not write speed more than once every 200 ms.
  The return keeps steps of a quarter of a percentage point to avoid
  dozens of nearly identical writes. The transport can also recover
  a deck at 1× whose clock gets stuck while the context keeps advancing.

The snapshot and the traces show the gain read from the AudioParam, in addition
to the targets, RMS before/after and execution technique. `mix.started`,
`mix.execution_fallback`, `mix.signal_fallback`, `mix.suspended` and
`mix.resumed` explain the state changes. The operator report shows
these events and measurements even outside the removal windows.

## November 7 follow-up: transient deficit and cuts in NORMAL

Three complete captures of the iPhone PWA, between 12:21 and 12:56
Europe/Madrid, contain 36 `clock_starved` alarms, 20 recovery cycles
and 16 recovery pauses. During the alarms there was data available, the context
was advancing, speed 1× and positive RMS before and after the fader. They fired
approximately one second after `playing`. The supervisor paused the
sources, suspended/resumed the context and wrote the position again;
a new start could expose the source to the same transient again.

Five DJ mixes show an initial deficit of 310–405 ms after a seek and
then stable progress. Their fallback, already at 1×, did not pause or restart the source,
but it declared speed control unavailable for future mixes.
These observations separate a transient from a sustained loss of progress;
they do not identify the internal iOS mechanism or certify the speakers.

Confirmation in two consecutive deficient windows is now required in the
shared observer, without adding recovery. Steps back keep their
immediate protection. Monitoring of a truly frozen context remains
separate and keeps its timings. A sustained source stall requires one
additional window before intervening; the cadence and availability of
observations determine the effective time, especially in the background.

Regressions: start/Play/seek with finite latency without pause, suspend or new
seeks; sustained deficit and frozen source; clearing of suspicion; DJ speed
protection at 1× and fallback when it really fails at variable speed.
A browser test delays only the counter while a real WAV passes through
the service and measures the program PCM for more than two windows. It checks
continuity, absence of health changes and progress, not only a resolved `play()`.

The fix does not change musical measurements, plans or the Android executor. The
physical acceptance of October 30 corresponds to that delivery. This
follow-up requires listening to NORMAL and DJ on the affected iPhone; it does not promise
to remove a possible initial browser latency.

Local validation of the follow-up: 1,885 UI/audio and type tests; 27 Python
trace tests. Full suites: Chromium mobile/desktop, 386 passed and 74
expected skips; WebKit mobile/desktop, 373 passed and 87 expected
skips (Docker, read-only mount, one worker). Fifteen targeted regressions
fail against the previous code; the browser test also fails
due to undue recovery with that code and passes with the fix. The first
broad Chromium run was discarded because of duplicate modules after switching
sources in Vite for that control; the previous full suite is the repeat
with a clean server and without source changes during the run.

## Connection

`connectionRecovery.ts` owns a single pending attempt and a timer
with a growing wait from one to thirty seconds. The initial identification
is recovered without reloading; `online`, `pageshow`, visibility and `resume`
allow an immediate attempt. It is cancelled on connecting or when its owner is destroyed;
manual retries share the pending request.

The initial library load uses the same retry owner; a
correct identification followed by a library failure does not leave the
screen empty indefinitely either. When the socket reconnects, a
library that had been left in error is requested again, keeping the previous snapshot.

Socket.IO keeps its own backoff, now without the five-attempt limit.
The runtime wakes the connection when the network or page recovers and removes those
listeners on exit. Initial identification failures leave up to eight
results without messages or addresses in sessionStorage; the authenticated
recorder collects them later. The server accepts only their
numeric/enumerated fields, together with the connection state changes.

## Validation and limits

- Regressions for partial clock, small accumulated steps back, variable
  speeds, pauses/seeks and timer suspension.
- Admission of PCM before/after the fader, protection of the outgoing track before and
  after dominance, 1× fallback in the next transition and recovery
  of a source at 1× with a live context.
- Three continuity regressions run against the previous code in
  an isolated worktree fail with the expected behavior; they pass with the
  new implementation.
- A fourth regression reproduces a file clock ahead of the
  fade: the early removal fails with the previous mixer and becomes
  tied to the output envelope with the common clock.
- Reconnection after more than five failures, concurrency, owner destruction,
  evidence privacy and real browser startup without reload.
- Measurement of real PCM before/after a fader in Chromium and WebKit. The
  general fixtures keep a long WAV; it now contains a faint tone,
  because digital silence does not prove admission of a song to the mixer.
- Full UI/audio and browser suites in the four profiles, plus
  Python validation of trace persistence/report. The final results
  are recorded in the PR and CI of the last head is required before delivery.

Final local validation: 1,825 UI/audio tests and 27 Python trace tests;
Ruff passes. Chromium mobile/desktop: 363 passed and 71 expected
skips. WebKit mobile/desktop: 354 passed and 80 expected skips
(Docker, one worker and read-only mount).

The later physical listening is accepted by the user (2026-10-30). These fallbacks mitigate observed
failures and allow diagnosing them; they do not certify the iOS renderer,
the Bluetooth route or the physical output of the car. The musical planning and
the early-exit decision for "Ambitions" are kept with their contract.
