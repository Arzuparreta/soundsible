# P13: phrases and sections over the measured bars

Physical acceptance (2026-10-30): the user confirms that the changes
work as expected in their real listening.

Before P13, `structure.py` counted fixed blocks of eight bars from the
first 1 of each region and labeled each block with hand-written thresholds
on its energy (0.9, 0.95, 1.18, 0.72, 0.35 and 0.48). Phrases and sections
came from the same assumption, so they matched each other without either
being measured. P13 measures them over the bars of P12-b and separates three things:

| Concept | Where it comes from | What is stored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The 1 of each bar | P12-b: Beat This! and its DBN, or whole bars counted from a 1 measured by the same beat (`projected_bar`) | `metric_regions`, `downbeats` |
| Phrase start | A measured change in the arrangement at a bar line, or whole bars counted from one | `phrases[]` with `evidence`: `boundary`, `counted` or `assumed` |
| Section and its role | Consecutive phrases with the same role, measured with levels in dB | `sections[]` with `label` (or `None`), `low_db`, `upper_db`, `starts_on_change` |

`ANALYSER_VERSION` goes from 11 to 12: the previous measurements are invalidated and the
library is analyzed again in the background. The contract with web and Android
does not change: `analysis_summary` omits `phrases` and `sections` as it previously omitted
`phrase_boundaries`, and the clients only execute the plan.

## Measurements

**Levels.** The 2048-sample FFT at 11 025 Hz that `features` already computed
for RMS and flatness now also gives the power of seven octave
bands (IEC 61260 centers from 63 Hz to 4 kHz; the last one ends at 5 kHz, like
the other measurements). The mean spectrum and the spectral flux of that FFT no longer
had readers and are removed. Each beat receives the level in dB of each band
(mean power of the frames whose center falls in the beat) and two aggregates:
the low band, 44–177 Hz (the two lowest octaves: kick drum and bass), and the
upper octaves, 177–5000 Hz. Each bar is the **median of its beats**:
an anacrusis beat (the kick drum that returns on the fourth beat) or the tail of a
hit does not move the change to another bar. This was seen in the prototype with music from
the library: in "Do To Me (Extended Mix)" the kick drum returns on the fourth beat
of bar 31 and bar 32 is already complete; with the mean of the frames the
change fell on 31, with the median of the beats on 32.

**Spans.** Bars are walked in continuous spans: contiguous regions
with model or projected ones. A span ends at a region without a bar
or projection, or at the gap between the head and the tail of a long recording.
The last bar of a span is only closed if the grid reaches its end
inside the measured coverage. Nothing is counted across a gap.

**Arrangement change.** At each bar line the four
previous bars are compared with the four following ones, band by band. There is a change if
in some band the difference of means is at least 3.01 dB (10·log₁₀ 2) and
exceeds three standard errors of those eight bars. The line with
the highest margin within ±3 bars is kept (the earlier one in a tie): two changes
are at least four bars apart and a ramp produces one change, not one every
few bars.

**Phrase length.** Per span: a length L divides at least two of the
intervals between consecutive changes, more than chance allows (an
interval is a multiple of L with probability 1/L). The binomial tail is
multiplied by the number of lengths tested (Bonferroni), from 4 bars
up to a quarter of the span, and must be below 0.0027 (three standard
errors, two-tailed). The smallest tail wins: changes every 16 bars give
16, not 8 or 4.

**Phrase starts.** Each change is a start (`boundary`). Between changes,
whole bars are counted from each one, and backwards from the first: with
the measured length (`counted`) or, without it, with eight bars (`assumed`). A
counted start less than four bars before the next change is omitted: that
change closes the phrase. This way an extension (8 + 8 + 10 + 8) or a phase that does not
start on the first 1 shifts the following phrases. A span without changes
counts eight bars from its first 1, as before, marked as assumed.

**Roles**, per segment between changes, with the low band compared with the
loudest segment of the song in that band:

| Beat state | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Without low beat | At least 10 dB below (less than half the loudness: it doubles every 10 phon), with more than three standard errors |
| With low beat | Less than 10 dB below, with more than three standard errors |
| Neither | Inherits the state of the previous segment of the same span, or none |

| Role | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| intro | Opening of the song: until the low beat starts or, if it opens with it, while each change only adds sound (some band rises and none falls) |
| outro | Closing: from where the low beat stops or, if it closes with it, from where each change only removes sound |
| breakdown | Without low beat |
| build | Without low beat, right before a drop, with the upper octaves rising at least 3.01 dB over the segment and a slope greater than three standard errors |
| drop | With low beat after a segment without it |
| body | With low beat, in the rest |
| `None` | Unknown state, one-bar segment or span without any measured change |

A level jump by itself does not produce a drop or a breakdown: everything 6 dB
louder or quieter leaves the low beat within 10 dB. Contiguous segments with
the same role form a section; a section boundary is a change of role.

**Why these constants are not taste.** 3.01 dB is doubling or halving
the power of a band: a sound enters with at least the power that was already
there, or half leaves. 10 dB is half the loudness. Four bars are
the four observations that `metre` requires for a repetition and the shortest
phrase that is resolved. Three standard errors and 0.0027 are the
repository convention. The bands are standardized octaves and the low one combines the two that
kick drum and bass occupy. Eight bars is the previous assumption: it is kept only
as a marked fallback. None was tuned with songs; the synthetic signals
of `tests/test_dj_structure.py` check each one against a known answer.

## Planner

- **Arrival at the drop** (`_drop_entry`): the end of the mix is searched first
  among measured phrase starts (`boundary` or `counted`), the farthest first;
  assumed ones only if no measured one fits. The incoming drop is a
  measured section (return of the low beat), not just a louder span.
- **Exit during the outro** (`_structural_exit`): a measured outro can be
  long. The exit can fall at its start or at any of its phrase
  starts, the one closest to the usual exit point. With only its start, an
  outro of half the song would have moved the mix forward to the middle of the track.
- **Before the breakdown** and **long mix** (`_section_run`) keep their
  logic over the new sections. `outro_cue` is still the first phrase
  start about 64 beats before the last sound.
- The phrases and sections of a song are not used as mutual confirmation:
  sections start at measured changes, which are also phrase starts,
  and no plan requires them to match.

## Evidence

`.venv/bin/python -I docs/internal/dj/structure_probe.py --model --library DIR --limit 60`
writes [p13-results.json](p13-results.json) (`ANALYSER_VERSION` 12). A change
only counts as a hit on its exact bar. The four measurements are
independent:

**1. Error of the structure itself** (exact model events, in its
20 ms frames; 96 bars per signal):

| Signals | Changes hit | Phrase length | Roles |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Phrases of 4, 8 and 16 bars from bars 4, 5 and 4; in 4/4, 3/4 and 6/8 (two dotted beats) | 117/117, none extra; median offset ≤ 0.2 ms | 4, 8 and 16 measured in the nine cases; correct phase (bar 5 is not a multiple of 8 from the first 1) | — |
| Extension 8 + 8 + 10 + 8 | 10/10 | 8, with the 10-bar phrase kept | — |
| Changes without a common length | 8/8 | none; eight `assumed` bars from each change | — |
| Intro, body, breakdown, drop and body; in 4/4, 3/4 and 6/8 | 15/15 | — | 100 % of the bars, no abstentions |
| Body, breakdown, build, drop and outro | 5/5 | — | 100 % |
| Everything 6 dB louder / quieter for 16 bars | 2/2 and 2/2 | — | 100 %: no drop or breakdown |
| Constant loop; melody 1.6 dB louder; fade of 0.5 dB per bar for 32 bars | 0 changes in all three | all `assumed` | all `None` (95 bars) |

**2. Error inherited from wrong bars** (same signals, altered
events): with all the 1s one beat late, the 11 changes appear at
+499.8 ms (exactly the error beat) and the phrase length is still 8;
the roles stay at 100 %. With three-beat bars over four-beat music,
the measured length becomes 11 model bars and 82.1 % of the bars
keep their role. The structure does not correct the 1: it inherits it as is.

**3. Real network** (Beat This! `final0` on the same signals at 22 050 Hz):
in 4/4, changes hit 22/22, 11/11 and 6/6 with one extra in the first two
signals, and lengths 4, 8 and 16; in the roles signal the whole grid is
half a beat early (−249 ms) and the changes with it, with the roles at 100 %.
In 3/4 and 6/8 the network groups the bars differently (P12-b: it reads 3/4 in groups
of four and 6/8 at 180 BPM duple); the structure inherits that error: changes
lost (0/22 in the four-bar phrases, which are no longer four
model bars apart) or false lengths (4 instead of 8). The roles stay
at 100 % in the three meters. Ambiguous material gives no changes or roles.

**4. Library, without annotations** (60 recordings of up to 8 minutes; 16
omitted because they last longer or less than 30 s): median of 10.1 changes per 100
bars. Of 644 intervals between consecutive changes, 44.6 % are multiples
of 4 bars (1/4 = 25 % if they were random) and 19.6 % of 8 (12.5 %); of 16,
3.4 %, below 1/16 because most intervals are short. The
phrase length is only established in 3 of 108 spans: in real music almost
all counted phrases are the eight-bar assumption from each change
(288 `assumed`, 39 `counted`, 736 `boundary`). Bars per role: body 5011,
intro 611, outro 543, drop 528, breakdown 511, build 12, without role 200. This is
agreement with phrasing practice, not precision: there is no annotated
reference.

**Planner and players.** `tests/test_dj_structure.py` checks with
these signals that the incoming drop ends on a measured phrase of the outgoing track
(bar 5 + 8n, where counting from the first 1 gave 8n), that the assumption still
serves as a fallback and that a span that is only louder is not taken as a drop. The
`phase` case of `test_measured_audio_sections_reach_route_and_refinement...`
carries the same through AAC → analysis with the network → SQLite → `dj-plan`,
`dj-transition`, `dj-place`, `dj-repair` and the legacy route, with the same plan in
all of them. `dj_structural_plans.json` adds `measured-phrase-phase` and
`measured-phrase-before-assumed` (the end of the mix at 218.25 s, a measured
phrase, where the farthest assumed one was 224.25 s); they are executed by the web store
and Android's `ProgramDjPlanTest` without moving points or shortening the overlap.

**Cost.** `_compose` on nine songs from the library (43.4 minutes,
same PCM and events, one thread): 0.067 s per minute of audio on `main` and 0.071
on this branch; traced memory peak 14.4 and 14.5 MiB. The network still costs
about 4 s per minute. The FFT of the measurements is the same; it only adds the per-band
sum and removes the mean spectrum and the flux.

## Limits

- Changes are measured with levels per octave band. A change that is only
  harmonic or in timbre with the same level per band (another progression with the
  same instruments) is not detected.
- Four-bar resolution: of two closer changes one is kept,
  and a shorter phrase is not resolved. A ramp slower than 3 dB every
  four bars does not produce a change; a faster one produces one inside it.
- Every error of the 1 or of the P12 grouping is inherited (measurements 2 and 3). In
  a block without a bar but with a stable beat, P12-b projects bars from the
  next 1 (`projected_bar`) even if the meter has changed inside the
  block; the structure counts over those bars.
- In real music the phrase length is almost never established: there are many
  small changes at different distances. The phrases between changes are
  then the eight-bar assumption, marked as such.
- An ending that only drops in level counts as outro even if it is long; that is why the
  exit can fall on any phrase of the outro, not only at its start. An
  opening without kick drum followed by its entry gives intro and drop.
- No structure annotations of real music, no comparison with another
  structure detector (All-In-One did not give a useful output in P12-a). This
  evidence is still pending; physical acceptance was confirmed by the
  user on 2026-10-30.
