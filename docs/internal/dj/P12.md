# P12: local metre and mixing guarantees

The contract distinguishes beat, grouping, subdivision and position of the first
beat. Old measurements are invalidated; clients receive explicit bars and
coverage.

**P12 is closed with P12-b (2026-10-26).** The heuristic detector of #394, which
is described below as historical, did not establish metre or downbeat in real music and
made tempo worse. The [P12-a comparison](DETECTOR_COMPARISON.md) led to
Beat This! `final0`; P12-b integrates it (next section). P13 counts phrases and
sections over these bars ([P13](P13.md)).

## P12-b: integrated detector

- **Beat and downbeats**: Beat This! `final0` (MIT) exported to ONNX and executed with
  ONNX Runtime, one thread and one inference at a time; log-mel frontend and chunking
  in NumPy (`shared/dj/beat_model.py`); 3/4-beat DBN ported from madmom to
  NumPy (`shared/dj/dbn.py`). Events equal to PyTorch + madmom on the 62
  inputs of P12-a. No PyTorch, madmom or SciPy in the engine.
- **Grid**: the time of each beat is read between frames at the activation
  peak; a line (or local curve, P1) weighted by the measured onset;
  phase and period corrected with onsets within ±20 ms. It only covers where the
  model heard beats. The P1 precision tests are still green.
- **Metre and downbeats** (`metre`): the model estimates them per region;
  `metre_confidence` and `downbeat_confidence` only stop being zero if the
  independent grouping (harmonic changes and accents, the one from #394) matches
  in size and phase on the same grid.
- **Mixing**: common beat cycle (`cycle_beats`, ratios 2ᵃ·3ᵇ: 1:1,
  2:1, 3:2, 4:3...), because reading in binary or in ternary is a choice of
  level. `alignment: bars` with confirmed downbeats and equal executed bars;
  `beats` with a stable beat in both, by user decision; otherwise, `none`.
  Web and Android only relocate by `relocation_seconds`: whole bars of
  the outgoing track that are also whole cycles.
- **Analysis**: one FFmpeg decode at 22050 Hz with LUFS; the rest of the
  measurements are computed at 11025 Hz after decimating in NumPy. `ANALYSER_VERSION`
  11. Without verified weights no analysis is stored.

Evidence: [detector_comparison/RESULTS](detector_comparison/RESULTS.md),
section P12-b, and `results/integrated-p12b.json`. On GTZAN, tempo within ±4 % in
24 of 30 recordings (#394: 10) and downbeat F1 0.81; the independent confirmation
does not appear in the real music measured, so its beatmatches declare `beats`;
pairs without executable alignment keep `none`.
The [revalidation of the final analyser](detector_comparison/FINAL_RESULTS.md)
records the per-case measurements, the revision and the executed hashes, the matrix
of pairs and the real analysis/cache/HTTP path after #395.

The following sections describe #394. Its contract remains in force except as
indicated above; its beat and grouping detector is historical.

## Measurements and evidence (#394)

`rhythm` keeps the temporal fit of P1. The autocorrelation considers
binary and ternary repetitions; the preference for the 120 BPM octave
disappears. Beat hypotheses with ratios 1, 1/2, 2, 2/3 and 3/2 are compared
through the normalized projection of the bass onsets onto each grid,
in 12-second windows to tolerate drift. The ternary ratios are
used to select the beat unit, not to stretch a 3:2 mix.
The tracking still uses all bands and resolves beat/offbeat
before fitting the onsets. The bass can accent another musical unit:
the real evaluation below measures that limit.

`metre` compares repeated profiles of 2, 3, 4 and 6 beats. It requires four
repetitions and a predictive improvement over a constant profile, leaving out
each repetition when predicting it. It separates harmonic change events by chroma
distance and onset level in dB; a harmonic cycle with large and
small changes does not necessarily double the bar. The grouping confidence is the
explained variance; the downbeat confidence depends on the separation between phases relative to the
observed variability. Three standard errors of separation are required. They are
evidence indices of the model, **not calibrated probabilities** of getting
the metre of a score right. Global key and regular tempo do not raise them.

The subdivision compares spectral onsets at halves and at both thirds of
each beat against the local background. Two patterns present can leave it
unknown, even if the beat and its grouping are clear. `pulse_unit` uses the
quarter note/dotted quarter note convention for binary/ternary subdivision; it is
`null` when that convention is not supported. Audio alone does not
identify a notated denominator and does not always distinguish a syncopation, a
hemiola and a change of unit. The executable unit is always `period`, in
seconds per point of `beat_grid`.

Each `metric_regions` keeps `start`, `end`, `period`, `beats_per_bar`,
`subdivision`, `pulse_unit`, `bar_seconds`, the four confidences and their
`downbeats`. Blocks of at least 24 beats are compared (up to 47 when distributing
the remainder). Only compatible adjacent estimates are merged; an uncertain
phase does not erase a neighbouring region with the identified downbeat. A block that
crosses a change can abstain, with a resolution of approximately one block.
Neither the gap between head/tail nor unknown bars are filled.
A uniform beat does not force inventing a metre.

`AnalysisSummary` omits the regions and all grids. It keeps the
grouping/subdivision/unit scalars and their confidences; a change or lack
of evidence can give `null`, and the confidences summarize the weakest evidence.
`bar_seconds` can also be `null`. The plan transmits only the two
local coverages needed to execute and relocate the cue.

## Planning and execution (#394)

A beatmatch technique requires a reliable beat, an allowed speed and
`alignment: bars`: both cues on measured downbeats, stable grids without gaps
during the whole overlap, and the same executed bar duration. The existing
5 ms budget is split between the two grids. The final checker
uses the rounded periods and speed of the contract. This guarantee covers
downbeats and the shared beat; it does not promise to align different subdivisions.

- Three beats of 0.5 s against four of 0.5 s do not keep all the downbeats:
  fallback at normal speed is chosen, without promising that alignment.
- Four beats of 0.5 s against two of 1 s can keep bars of
  2 s and a shared beat. Half/double tempo folding does not multiply or
  divide the measured bar.
- `out_bar_seconds` and `in_bar_seconds` are local; `out_metre` and `in_metre`
  contain units, grouping, subdivision, confidences and coverage in
  media seconds. `overlap_bars` counts measured outgoing bars; it is
  `null` if there is no measurement for that segment.
- Fallback techniques declare `alignment: none`. The previous score is
  kept for executable beatmatch and is zero when the metric contract
  requires fallback. It is a validity requirement, without adding taste weights.
- Sources, legacy HTTP with new analysis, ordering, placement, requests,
  repair and refinement use the same plan. `beatmatch_fits` also validates
  the bridges: all their segments, including the last one, must be executable.
  An average of scores cannot hide an incompatible arrival.
- The echo still repeats a measured outgoing beat at the cue, in media
  seconds; the executors correct it for the real speed. An unmeasured gap
  cannot supply an echo beat or a downbeat from another window.

Web and Android read the explicit bar. If they must relocate the cue, they move it
by complete bars within the valid coverage for **the whole** new
overlap. Insufficient coverage, inconsistent fields or the lack of a valid whole
position make the beatmatch fall back to `structural_fade`, at
normal speed. Android keeps the contract when serializing the session.

Compatibility is delimited: only an old plan without the metric
fields keeps the four-beat fallback. An `out_bar_seconds: null` is
an explicit unknown, not permission to reconstruct it. Measurements without
`metric_regions` keep the legacy policy for old consumers;
the P11-a discrepancy remains documented for that input. The current cache
already delivers the new contract. The old vectors are not regenerated to
make them pass: eight metric vectors executed by the server,
web store and native resolution are added, including displacement and degradation.
When closing P11-a that compatibility is removed from the server and the web, which
never reached it in production, and the old vectors are regenerated with
metric regions; Android keeps it for stations older than P12.

## Reproducible evidence

The probe of #394 measured its heuristic detector, replaced in P12-b; it is no longer
in `main`. To reproduce [p12-results.json](p12-results.json), in a worktree
of `2ca8ccf2`:

```sh
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/pytest -q tests/test_dj_metre.py tests/test_discovery_save_route.py
.venv/bin/python -I docs/internal/dj/metre_probe.py
.venv/bin/python -I docs/internal/dj/metre_probe.py --ballroom /path/to/BallroomData
```

The last command reads the twelve files pinned in [p12-results.json](p12-results.json)
and downloads only their annotations at a pinned revision. It also accepts
a JSON manifest with `file`, `genre`, `annotation`. It uses a temporary SQLite;
it does not read or alter the application cache. The individual audio
and annotation hashes are in the result. The Ballroom audio is obtained from the
[original archive](https://mtg.upf.edu/ismir2004/contest/tempoContest/data1.tar.gz);
it is not redistributed in this repository.

The tests cover 3/4, 4/4 and 6/8 with several periods, initial positions,
anacruses, weak first beats, offbeats, coexisting subdivisions,
half/double tempo, changes inside/outside a block, drift, silence/tone and
beat without grouping. The temporal tolerances come from the synthesized
onsets; they are not tuned with notes from songs. The AAC → SQLite →
HTTP chain verifies an ignored previous cache, cold state, single acquisition, warm
reads without decoding, compact summary and all endpoints.

The probe separates tempo error, beat error, grouping, subdivision, downbeat
error and abstentions. It evaluates only between the first and last reference beat,
to exclude unannotated intros. On stable 120 BPM signals of the probe, the
error is at most 0.001 BPM and the 90th percentile of the beat stays below
1 ms. With an amplitude drift of 1.5 %, the 90th percentile stays below 4 ms;
fixed-speed beatmatch is not promised for them. The full result
includes the ambiguous cases and the local confidences.

## Real evaluation and limits (#394)

The first three files found in the original archive of
each of four styles were chosen before comparing their results. The
detector was not calibrated with them. The references are the annotations of
[BallroomAnnotations](https://github.com/CPJKU/BallroomAnnotations/tree/826c2eadb7d89e8fbcc655477416f8b3d3ff1c0e),
Krebs, Böck and Widmer, ISMIR 2013. They are manually corrected annotations with a
beat level conditioned by the dance style; they are an
explicit and reviewable reference, not a universal truth about the beat. The subdivision is not
annotated: it is not scored as a real hit.

**In the twelve excerpts no correct joint coverage of metre and
downbeat is established.** There are groupings of two beats against four annotated,
unknown regions and phases without sufficient separation. The plan does not promise
bar alignment in them. There are octave errors in the slow waltzes and
2:3 ratios in the Viennese ones; a precise periodic grid does not establish a
correct musical interpretation. These are detection failures and abstentions,
not a real improvement demonstrated by the synthetic cases.
