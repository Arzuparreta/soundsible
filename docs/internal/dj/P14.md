# P14. First mix and work duration

Physical acceptance (2026-10-30): the user confirms that the changes
work as expected in their real listening.

User decision, 2026-10-28: DJ work belongs only to current
sessions. The cache helps the sessions; it does not justify continuing discarded
sessions. Musical measurements and `ANALYSER_VERSION` do not change.

## Diagnosis

A local session created a route at 15:41:18, refined ten times between 15:43:13 and
15:43:33 and committed a fallback at 15:43:34. The six-second fade
happened at 15:44:19–25; the incoming analysis arrived at 15:44:44 even though
the audio had been in the cache for two days. Replanning with both measurements gives a
valid `bass_swap` at about 128 BPM and 15 seconds. There were two simultaneous causes:

- A global FIFO analysis queue: discarded sessions, library sweep
  and NORMAL previews competed with the songs that were actually going to play.
- The clients only refined between 65 and 45 seconds before the cue; arming a
  fallback closed the possibility of adopting a later measurement. Promoting
  A→B→C to A→C also left the predecessor B in the plan.

Also, 16 candidates were acquired for routes of eight or refills of four,
without reusing in that route the new measurements of the discarded ones. There were
URL resolutions with full bytes already available, duplicated extractions
between DJ and Discover and playlists enumerated before applying the requested limit.

## Current contract

- `X-Soundsible-Work` identifies client and epoch, within account and runtime.
  A new epoch retires the previous one; an explicit close cannot be resurrected
  by a delayed request. Other devices/accounts keep their demands.
- `dj-work` publishes with an increasing revision the current song and the next
  eight accepted ones. Removing a song releases its analysis,
  download and resolution interests. Planning, placing or repairing only queries the available
  measurements; prospective candidates do not acquire audio.
- Planning requests carry an ID captured with their epoch.
  Aborting also cancels on the server. When a route is returned, searches
  that will no longer contribute to that response are released. An empty response that announces
  `warming` keeps the seeds for the retry: an identical request
  transfers its demand before closing the previous one; a different direction cancels it.
- The last consumer cancels queued jobs and releases their capacity. If the
  analysis is already running, that exchange of the child process is killed and the next one
  starts clean. In the gevent engine the network wait is also interrupted;
  tools with ordinary threads use cancellation points and closes.
  Cancelling does not mark the recording as defective or write a partial analysis.
  Calls that are still running inside the HTTP request are also
  interrupted when their demand is removed; they cannot continue to another fallback
  search after the planning is cancelled.
- Shared resources continue while another consumer needs them,
  including a direct playback reader. A completed preview only
  requests analysis for the DJs that are still interested. Audio and full measurements
  keep their current caches; they are not duplicated per session.
- Clients renew demand every 15 seconds. A disconnection without close
  expires at 120 seconds; the reaper removes it within up to ten more seconds.
  Resuming an expired session allows renewing its same epoch; one closed
  explicitly requires a new epoch. A process that disappears without being able to send
  its close is not detected instantly.
- The web preview preparation also publishes revisions and interests in
  the prefetch/status requests: an old packet does not reopen removed
  songs. Old clients use compatibility demand with expiration;
  they do not promise immediate cancellation on exit.

The DJ sweep of all accounts, the Discover warm-up at
startup/library change and the warm-up of result URLs from the DJ explorer are removed.
General search does not speculate while the DJ is active. DJ and Discover
share extraction and cache per seed; extractions without an explicit Discover
owner have a maximum demand of 30 seconds. Playlist
limits are applied before extracting and only the requested number is consumed.
Both prefetch paths avoid resolving a URL if the audio is complete.
Fill telemetry uses the existing `play-timing` category.

## First mix

Web and Android refine from the start, with bounded retries, without waiting
for the old twenty-second window. If the measurement arrives with the incoming
deck armed but still silent, they can replace the fallback as long as
more than five seconds remain until the new cue. A mix
that is already playing is not replaced and the playback preparation is not torn down with mixing
disabled. Refinement also continues during the loading of the incoming deck.
A change of the successor repairs its predecessor and refines again.

Priority is given by demand and real playback order, without inserting a special
job that leaves obsolete songs behind. Beatmatch is not guaranteed if the
network, the initial model or the analysis do not finish before the cue, or if the music
does not allow one. A fallback is still correct in those cases.
